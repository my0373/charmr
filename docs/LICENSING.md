# Why This Licensing Model?

## The short version

Charmr is free for charities. Always. No strings attached.

The licence exists to keep it that way — and to prevent companies from taking this software, wrapping it in a subscription, and selling it back to the very charities it was built to help.

## The problem we're solving

Charities operate on tight budgets. When good software is released under a traditional open-source licence, there is nothing stopping a commercial company from taking it, hosting it, and charging charities a monthly fee to use it. The charity ends up paying for something that was built for them, by people who wanted them to have it for free.

This has happened repeatedly in the open-source world. Projects built with the best of intentions end up as the foundation of someone else's revenue stream, while the original creators and intended users see none of the benefit.

We don't think that's right.

## What charities can do

Everything. Charities, non-profits, community groups, educational institutions, academic researchers, students, and individuals can:

- Use the software for free, forever
- Modify it to suit their needs
- Host it anywhere — on a laptop, a server, AWS, Azure, a Raspberry Pi, wherever works
- Run it on the public internet so members, donors, and supporters can access it
- Collect donations, sell merchandise, charge for events, and run fundraising campaigns through it
- Use it for academic research, coursework, teaching, or university-affiliated projects
- Ask someone else to host and manage it for them — a volunteer, a contractor, an IT company, whoever they trust

That last point is important. We do not want charities to feel they have to be their own IT department. If a charity wants to pay someone to set it up and keep it running, that is absolutely fine. The provider does not need a special licence for that. It's the charity's instance, run for the charity's benefit.

## What companies cannot do

The only thing this licence restricts is commercial exploitation of the software itself:

- A company cannot take Charmr, host it as a multi-tenant SaaS platform, and charge people to use it
- A company cannot white-label it and sell it as their own product
- A company cannot bundle it into a commercial offering for resale

If a company wants to do any of these things, they need to talk to us first and get a commercial licence. We're not opposed to commercial use — we just want to make sure it's done fairly and that the project benefits from it.

## Why not a traditional open-source licence?

Traditional open-source licences like the GPL, MIT, or Apache 2.0 are excellent for many projects. But they all have the same gap: they cannot prevent a company from taking the software and selling it as a service.

Licences like the AGPL try to address this by requiring anyone who hosts the software to release their modifications, but they don't prevent commercial hosting outright. A company can comply with the AGPL and still charge charities to use the software.

The Charity First Licence takes a different approach. It says: if you are a charity, a non-profit, or an academic, you can do whatever you want. If you are a company looking to make money from the software, come and talk to us.

This makes Charmr "source-available" rather than "open source" in the strict definition. The source code is public, contributions are welcome, and charities can use it without restriction. The only difference is that commercial exploitation requires permission.

## The bottom line

This software was built to save charities time and money. The licence exists to make sure that promise holds — not just today, but permanently. Charities should be able to use Charmr without fear, without hidden costs, and without worrying that someone is going to turn their tool into a product and charge them for the privilege.
